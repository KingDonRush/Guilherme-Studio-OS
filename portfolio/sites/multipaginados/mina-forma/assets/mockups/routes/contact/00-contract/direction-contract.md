# Contact direction contract

## Route job

`/contact` must let a visitor send a useful first note or choose a direct channel without requiring a finished brief. It must not duplicate the structured scope-builder owned by `/plan-your-project`.

## Direction

- **Audience moment:** the visitor wants to make contact but may know only part of the context.
- **Memorable truth:** a simple project note is enough to begin.
- **Primary action:** `SEND MESSAGE`.
- **First three eye stops:** the Contact statement; the simple message form; the direct contact-channel rail.
- **Sibling relationship:** Home positions, Services explains the offer, Projects proves the work, About explains the studio, Contact opens a conversation, Plan your project structures a detailed request.
- **Anti-pattern:** Home-like split hero; a second quote-builder; three equal contact cards; oversized decorative photography; a dense project-intake questionnaire.

## Section map

1. Compact shared header with `Contact` active.
2. Image-free editorial masthead with one statement and one support paragraph.
3. Primary action surface: simple form on the left; direct channels and optional context prompts on the right.
4. Human pause: one restrained planning-desk photograph paired with `A SIMPLE NOTE IS ENOUGH`.
5. Studio presence: `SÃO PAULO, BRAZIL` beside abstract location line-art, without a fictional street address.
6. Four quick-question accordion rows.
7. Dark bridge to the detailed planning route.
8. Shared compact footer.

## Content budget

- Masthead: one eyebrow, one three-line headline and one paragraph; no button.
- Form: name, email, company/brand and message only.
- Channels: email, WhatsApp and LinkedIn as three flat rows.
- Context prompts: space type, current stage, timeline and known constraints; guidance only, not required inputs.
- Human pause: one heading, one sentence and one photograph.
- Presence: city, service reach and appointment note; no street address.
- FAQ: four collapsed questions.
- Planning bridge: one label, one headline, one sentence and one button.

## Primary evidence

The editable contact form and direct-channel rail are the primary evidence. Photography only gives the functional surface a human, tactile pause.

## Elementor map

- Header/footer: shared Theme Builder templates; WordPress Nav Menu for real navigation.
- Masthead: Containers, Heading and Text Editor widgets.
- Form: Elementor Form widget with four fields and one submit button.
- Channels/context prompts: nested Containers with Heading/Text Editor; use Icon List only if native icons improve recognition.
- Human pause: Image, Heading and Text Editor widgets in a regular two-column Container.
- Presence: Image widget for map line-art plus Heading/Text Editor; no embedded live map is implied.
- FAQ: Elementor Accordion widget, closed by default.
- Planning bridge: dark Container, Heading, Text Editor and Button.

## Treatment budgets

- **Border contract:** outer rails, section separators, form-field outlines and accordion rows are mandatory; no card frames around every contact item.
- **Shadow budget:** zero interface shadows; only natural shadows inside the photograph.
- **Texture budget:** subtle paper field globally; material texture appears only inside the planning photograph.
- **Image density:** one photograph plus one quiet line-art map across the complete page.
- **CTA emphasis:** `SEND MESSAGE` is the primary orange action; `PLAN YOUR PROJECT` is the secondary route bridge.

## Prohibited drift

- No Home-style text/photo split hero.
- No price, quantity, cart, proposal total or quote-builder control from Plan your project.
- No Belo Horizonte label or fictional street address.
- No project-type/timeline form expansion beyond the four locked fields.
- No three equal channel cards, floating panels, rounded corners, gradients, fake metrics or dashboard UI.
- No fragile overlap or absolute positioning; all content maps to normal Elementor containers.
